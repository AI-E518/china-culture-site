The single biggest "why is my phone broken?" moment for first-time visitors to China is the **Great Firewall**. At the airport you'll find 5G is fast and cheap — but Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, and X/Twitter won't open without a workaround. The fix is simple if you plan it **before you fly**, and a headache if you don't.

## Option 1 — International eSIM (recommended)
An eSIM is a downloadable SIM profile. You buy a China data plan from a provider, install it at home over Wi-Fi, and switch it on when you land. You're online before you've left the terminal.

Why it's the easy choice:
- **No passport photocopy, no kiosk hunt.** Install at home.
- **Foreign apps keep working** — most travel eSIMs route your traffic through Hong Kong, Singapore, or Europe, so Google Maps, WhatsApp, Gmail, and Instagram open normally. No VPN needed.
- **Dual-SIM friendly** — keep your home SIM active for calls while the eSIM handles data.

Popular 2026 providers: **Airalo** (budget, China Unicom network, HK routing), **Holafly** (flat-rate unlimited, 24/7 chat, international routing), **Nomad** (mid-range, good for multi-country Asia trips), plus Maya Mobile, GigSky, and KnowRoaming for business needs. A typical 5–10 day trip needs about 3–5 GB; heavy video calls eat ~1 GB a day.

> Before you buy, check the plan says it reaches **outside** the firewall (look for "HK routing," "international routing," or "access Google/WhatsApp"). A cheap plan that gives you a Chinese IP will still block those apps.

## Option 2 — Local data SIM
Buy at an airport kiosk or a carrier store (China Mobile, China Unicom, China Telecom, China Broadnet) with your passport — real-name registration is required by law. Costs are low and speeds are excellent, **but you get a Chinese IP**, which means Google/WhatsApp/Gmail are blocked unless you add a VPN. Best if you'll mostly use Chinese apps (Alipay, WeChat, Amap) and don't need foreign services.

## Option 3 — Roaming
Your home carrier's roaming works for calls and data, but it's usually slower and far more expensive. Keep it as a backup, not your main plan.

## Get your phone ready (before departure)
- [ ] Phone is **carrier-unlocked** (contract phones often aren't — unlock it 24–72h ahead).
- [ ] Phone supports eSIM: iPhone XS or newer, Pixel 3+, Galaxy S20+ and most recent flagships.
- [ ] Buy and **install** the eSIM at home on Wi-Fi; leave it off until you land.
- [ ] Download offline maps (Amap or Baidu) and a translator so you're never fully stranded.

## What to use instead of blocked apps
- **Maps**: Amap (高德) or Baidu Maps — Google Maps is unreliable in China.
- **Chat**: WeChat (works internationally too).
- **Translation**: Pleco (offline Chinese dictionary) or your phone's built-in translator; Baidu Translate has camera mode.
- **Email**: if Gmail is blocked on a mainland connection, use the eSIM route above or your carrier's roaming.

Sort connectivity at home, land with data already on, and China's internet stops being a wall and becomes just… the internet.
