China's entry rules changed more in 2024–2026 than in the previous decade combined. The headline: **for most short-term visitors, a visa is no longer the first thing you should worry about.** Three separate routes now let foreign passport holders in without a traditional embassy visa — and together they cover the large majority of leisure and business trips.

This guide breaks down the three ways in, exactly what to prepare, and the avoidable mistakes we still see people make.

## The three ways in (no embassy visa)

### 1. 240-hour (10-day) transit visa-free — the big one for long-haul flyers
If your itinerary includes China as a **stopover on the way to a third country or region**, you likely qualify. As of August 2026 the program covers **57 countries** (recently expanded to include Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam) and **65 open ports** across China.

What you need:
- A passport valid for at least 6 months.
- A **confirmed onward ticket** to a third country/region within 240 hours (10 days) of arrival. The destination must be different from where you came from.
- Entry and exit through designated ports (major international airports and a number of land/sea ports).

You may travel across permitted regions during the stay — great for a Beijing–Xi'an–Shanghai swing. Tourism, business, exchanges, and family visits are fine; employment, study, and journalism still require the proper visa.

### 2. Unilateral visa-free entry — for tourism, no transit required
China unilaterally waives visas for ordinary passport holders of roughly **50 countries**, currently valid through **December 31, 2026**. This covers most of the EU (France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Switzerland, and more), plus Japan, South Korea, the UK, Canada, and others, for stays up to 30 days. No onward ticket needed — just show up.

### 3. Mutual visa exemption — standing agreements
About **29 countries** have reciprocal visa-free agreements with China (e.g., Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, UAE, Qatar, Serbia, Kazakhstan). These are long-standing and not tied to the 2026 pilots.

> **Quick check:** If your country appears in routes 2 or 3, you can visit for tourism without a visa and without a connecting flight. If not, route 1 (transit) is your path — pair China with a side trip to, say, Japan, South Korea, or Hong Kong/Macau.

## Before you fly — checklist
- [ ] Passport valid **6+ months** past entry.
- [ ] For transit entry: **confirmed onward/return ticket** to a third country/region.
- [ ] First 2–3 nights of hotel addresses handy (you'll fill an arrival card).
- [ ] A screenshot of the official policy notice for your country.
- [ ] The **12367** service hotline saved — it answers in Mandarin, Cantonese, English, Japanese, Korean, and Russian.

## Mistakes that still get people turned away
1. **Wrong "third country."** Flying New York → Shanghai → New York does **not** count as transit; the onward leg must leave the China region for a different country/region.
2. **Expired window.** Arriving with a ticket 241 hours out misses the 240-hour limit. Build in buffer.
3. **Assuming every port qualifies.** Only the 65 designated ports accept transit entry. A tiny regional airport may not.
4. **Trusting a blog screenshot from 2023.** Rules shifted yearly. Verify on the National Immigration Administration site or a Chinese embassy page within a week of flying.

## If you do need a visa
Some trips still require one — long stays, work, study, journalism, or citizens of countries outside the three routes above. Apply at a Chinese embassy or consulate, or via an authorized visa center. Build in 2–4 weeks.

China wants you here. The paperwork is lighter than it's ever been — just match your passport to the right route before you book the flight.
