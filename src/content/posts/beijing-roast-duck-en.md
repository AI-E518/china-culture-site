The first time I took a foreign friend for roast duck, he froze while watching the chef carve the bird: “Wait—this requires over 100 precise cuts?”  
—Yes. The meticulous art of Beijing roast duck begins long before the duck reaches your table—with its very origin.

## What It Is  

Beijing roast duck boasts a history of over 600 years and comes in two main styles: *guàlú* (hanging-oven roasted over open fruitwood fire) and *mènlú* (closed-oven roasted). It demands specially raised Pekin ducks, scalded skin, caramelized glaze, and slow roasting over fragrant fruitwood. The roasting techniques of Quanjude and Bianyifang are both inscribed on China’s National Intangible Cultural Heritage list. When served, the crisp skin is expertly sliced, dipped in sweet bean sauce, layered with scallion shreds and cucumber strips, then wrapped in thin pancakes—a ritualistic sequence that feels like a performance in itself.

## My Take  

My wife and I once treated “tasting every notable Beijing roast duck” as a serious mission. From humble neighborhood *guàlú* stalls charging just over ¥100 per set, to refined restaurants where a single set costs well over ¥1,000, we worked our way through them all. Over time, I’ve lost track of which restaurant served what exact flavor—but one image remains vivid: the two of us, a fragrant duck fresh from the oven, a warm pancake rolled tight, slowly savoring the city, bite by bite, day by day. Eventually, I realized—the greatness of roast duck lies not in price or prestige, but in *who* you’re with, *when* you sit down together, and *how* you wait—side by side—as the chef finishes carving that duck.

## Why Foreigners Fall in Love  

Roast duck transforms dining into both spectacle and ceremony—much like French foie gras or Japanese kaiseki—and thus conveys “Chinese refinement” in a language foreigners instantly understand. And unlike spicy or unfamiliar flavors, it’s approachable: nearly every foreigner accepts the first bite without hesitation—only to be utterly won over by that shatteringly crisp skin.

## Where to Go · How to Begin  

Skip the tourist traps. Seek out a *guàlú* restaurant beloved by locals—such as Siji Minfu, Da Dong, or the time-honored Bianyifang. Order a full set, watch the chef carve the duck tableside, and pair it with a glass of *erguotou* baijiu. Beijing is absolutely worth a special trip—for one duck alone.
