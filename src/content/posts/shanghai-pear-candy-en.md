Many foreigners know Shanghai’s xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) and shengjian bao (pan-fried buns), but few realize: when Shanghainese children had sore throats, they didn’t suck Western lozenges—they dissolved a sweet piece of *Ligao Tang* (pear-syrup candy).

## What Is It?

*Ligao Tang* is a traditional Shanghai confection passed down for over a century in the city’s historic Old City. Made by slowly simmering pear juice and rock sugar with herbal ingredients—including platycodon root, mint, and licorice—it began as a soothing throat remedy before evolving into a beloved snack for all ages. At the Old City God Temple (Yuyuan Garden) bazaar, master artisans still prepare it on-site: boiling the syrup in copper kettles, cutting it into squares, and calling out to passersby—a quintessential Shanghai street scene.

## My Take

I’ve lived in Shanghai for nearly thirty years, and *Ligao Tang* has always been a “love-hate” presence in my life. As a child, I genuinely disliked its flavor—I couldn’t quite name what felt “off,” but I refused to let it dissolve under my tongue. Later, as work demands brought frequent speaking and recurring throat irritation, something shifted: one day—no idea which—I tried a piece when my voice turned hoarse… and found it worked better than imported throat lozenges—refreshingly clean, never cloying or heavy.

And just like that, it transformed—from a candy I’d dodge as a kid to a staple in my bag. One little square holds three decades of my Shanghai life: sweetness laced with the gentle, grounding bitterness of herbs.

## Why Foreigners Fall for It

It turns “medicine” into “candy”—functionally equivalent to Western cough drops, yet wholly natural and brimming with artisanal warmth. To outsiders, it’s an endearing glimpse into how Chinese people nurture themselves through food. Compact, lightweight, and sturdy (won’t crumble in your pocket), it’s the perfect “gateway” Chinese product—portable, giftable, and virtually impossible to find authentically outside China.

## Where to Go · How to Start

Head to the historic *Ligao Tang* shops at Shanghai’s Old City God Temple (Yuyuan Garden). Pick up classic or mint-flavored varieties as souvenirs—and look for the authentic “Old City God Temple” brand logo; many street-side imitations exist. A few boxes make a far more thoughtful and culturally rich gift than any souvenir keychain.
