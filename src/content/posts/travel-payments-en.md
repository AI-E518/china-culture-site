China is one of the most cashless societies on earth. Walk into a downtown restaurant, scan a code, and you're done — no wallet, no card swipe. For visitors, the good news is that **since 2023 you no longer need a Chinese bank account**: both Alipay and WeChat Pay now bind international cards directly.

This guide gets you from "I just landed" to "I paid for dumplings with my phone" — with the limits and gotchas spelled out.

## Step 1 — Install and register
- Download **Alipay** (look for the international version with a globe icon and full English UI) and **WeChat** from your app store before you fly. Doing it at home avoids any setup surprises on Chinese networks.
- Register with your **home phone number** (country code + number). No Chinese number needed.
- In WeChat, set up your account early — brand-new accounts occasionally trigger an extra security check, easier to clear from abroad.

## Step 2 — Verify your identity (passport)
Both apps require real-name verification for foreign cards:
- **Alipay**: *Me → Settings → Identity Verification* → upload a clear photo of your passport data page and take a selfie.
- **WeChat Pay**: *Me → Services → Wallet → Cards* → add card, then complete passport verification when prompted.

This is an official requirement and it unlocks the normal spending limits. Have your passport in hand.

## Step 3 — Add your international card
- **Alipay**: *Me → Bank Cards → Add Card*, then enter Visa / Mastercard / Amex / JCB / Discover / Diners details.
- **WeChat Pay**: *Me → Services → Wallet → Cards → Add a Card*. The same card can live in both apps.
- Add **two cards from different networks** if you can. Issuer acceptance varies, and a declined card is the most common setup failure. Also set a travel notice with your bank so it doesn't block the charge.

## Two QR moves you need to know
- **Scan (你扫商户 / "I scan you")**: tap *Scan*, point at the merchant's QR code, enter the amount.
- **Be scanned (商户扫你 / "you're scanned")**: open your payment code (Alipay *Pay*, WeChat *Money*) and let the cashier scan it.

Learn both before you land — payments then take seconds.

## Fees, limits & rules (2026)
- **Fee**: free for single transactions of **¥200 or less**; **3%** on the whole amount above ¥200.
- **Limit**: foreign cards capped at about **USD 5,000 per transaction** and **USD 50,000 per year**.
- **What you can't do**: person-to-person transfers, red packets (红包), and financial products are off-limits to foreign cards. Those need a local bank account.
- **Alipay Tour Pass** (optional): a prepaid top-up from your foreign card that can avoid some foreign-exchange fees your bank might charge. Find it via in-app search.

## When the card won't bind — fallbacks
- **Carry a little cash**: a few hundred RMB covers market stalls and the rare dead spot. Airport ATMs accept major foreign cards; your hotel can break large notes.
- **Tell your bank you're traveling** and enable international transactions — most "it declined" moments are the issuer, not the app.
- **Try the other app**: if Alipay rejects a card, WeChat often works, and vice versa.

> **One-line emergency phrase**: "这里能用支付宝吗？" (*Can you take Alipay?*) — say it, and you'll know in one word whether to scan or reach for cash.

Set both apps up at home, verify your passport, and by the time you're in line for xiaolongbao you'll be the person who pays in two seconds while everyone else fumbles.
