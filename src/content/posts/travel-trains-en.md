China's high-speed rail is the best way to travel between cities — clean, punctual, and faster than flying once you count airport time. The network spans the country, and **foreign passport holders can book it entirely online**. The only wrinkle is the real-name system, which is easier than it sounds if you set it up right.

## Route 1 — 12306 (official, cheapest)
12306 is China Railway's official platform. The app and website have **full English versions**, and passports are a supported ID type.

Setup:
1. Download the **12306 app** or visit the English site (12306.cn/en).
2. Register and choose **passport** as your ID type. Enter your passport number, name exactly as printed, nationality, and date of birth.
3. **Verify your identity**: upload a photo of your passport data page. Review is usually completed within about 24 hours. If it stalls, take the passport to any station ticket window for an instant manual check.
4. Search trains (English station names work), pick a class — **second class (二等座)** is comfortable and standard, **first class (一等座)** roomier, **business (商务座)** lie-flat.
5. Pay with Alipay, WeChat, or an international card (Visa / Mastercard / JCB / Diners).

On travel day: bring the **original passport**. China uses electronic tickets — at most stations you can now swipe your passport at the gate; elsewhere use the manual channel. Allow time for security screening.

> **Name matching tip**: enter your name exactly as on the passport, paying attention to surname/given-name order. A mismatch is the #1 reason bookings fail.

## Route 2 — Trip.com (easiest for visitors)
Trip.com (Ctrip) offers an **English interface**, accepts **foreign cards**, and handles the name-format quirks for you. It adds a small per-ticket service fee, but for many first-timers the simplicity is worth it. Good when 12306's strict name matching gives you trouble.

## Route 3 — Station window (reliable fallback)
Any railway station ticket office sells to foreigners with a passport, in cash or via Alipay/WeChat. Use this if apps reject your document or you're buying last-minute.

## Practical pointers
- **Book early for holidays**: Chinese New Year, National Day (early October), and summer peak sell out within hours. Tickets open about **15 days** ahead.
- **Carry the booking passport**: the ticket is tied to that document; a different passport won't match.
- **An itinerary sheet is not a ticket**: the 12306 confirmation email/receipt alone won't get you through the gate — you need the passport (and, at some stations, a printed/QR ticket).
- **Quiet carriages** are available on many trains if you want a calm ride; some trains also offer "pet-friendly" and luggage-forwarding services on select routes.

## A note on planes
For very long hauls (e.g., Beijing to Guangzhou or Chengdu), flying can be faster. Book via airline apps or Trip.com, paying with Alipay/WeChat or a card. The same passport-real-name rule applies.

Set up 12306 before you fly, verify your passport, and city-hopping becomes a 2-hour, 300 km/h pleasure rather than a logistics puzzle.
