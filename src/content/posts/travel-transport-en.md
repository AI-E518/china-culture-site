faq:
- q: 在中国坐地铁最简单的方式是什么？
  a: 用支付宝或微信里的乘车码（城市交通小程序）扫码进出，不用买纸质票。必要时出示护照。
- q: 滴滴安全吗？对外国人友好吗？
  a: 安全且友好。滴滴是本地版 Uber，可用支付宝/微信支付，有英文界面。目的地可在地图上标点，或从酒店复制中文名称粘贴。
- q: 交通类 App 能用外卡吗？
  a: 支付宝/微信的乘车码绑定外卡即可用。单独的共享单车 App 可能需要中国手机号，所以单车是可选项。
- q: 适合走路吗？
  a: 市中心、老城区、滨水地带很适合步行。用高德看步行路线，注意非机动车道里的电动车。
- q: 从机场怎么到市区？
  a: 多数大机场有地铁接驳（如北京机场快轨、上海地铁 2 号线 / 磁悬浮），也可打滴滴。打车请走官方排队区、坚持打表，别理非法拉客。

Chinese cities are built for moving — dense metro networks, cheap rides, and walkable cores. Once you know the three moves below, getting around feels like any other big city, just faster and cheaper.

## Move 1 — Metro (the backbone)
China has metro systems in **55+ cities**. The trick for visitors: **you don't buy a ticket — you scan a QR code from your phone.**

- Open **Alipay** or **WeChat** → find the **Transit / 出行** mini-program → select the city → complete one-time identity verification → you get a metro/bus QR code.
- Scan it at the gate to enter and exit. Fares are distance-based and cheap (a cross-town ride is often under ¥10).
- At some stations you can also buy a single-journey token at a machine, but the QR code is faster and avoids cash.
- **Peak hours** (roughly 7:30–9:30 and 17:30–19:30) get crowded — build in buffer time.

> If the transit mini-program asks for a local number you don't have, use the station's token machines or a staffed window for a paper ticket.

## Move 2 — DiDi (ride-hailing)
DiDi is China's Uber, and it's the safest, most predictable way to get a car.
- Switch the app to **English mode**; pay via Alipay or WeChat (bound international card works).
- Set your destination by **pinning it on the map** or pasting the **Chinese name** from your hotel — English names often don't resolve well in the app.
- **Confirm the plate number** on the app matches the car before you get in.
- For the airport, use the **official taxi queue** and insist the driver uses the meter; licensed taxis are metered, and unlicensed touts at arrivals are best avoided.

## Move 3 — Shared bikes & walking
- **Shared bikes** (Meituan, Hello, DiDi bikes) are everywhere. Standalone bike apps may require a Chinese phone number, so treat them as optional. Great for short hops along bike lanes.
- **Walking** is genuinely pleasant in old districts, pedestrian streets, and riversides. Use **Amap** for walking routes, and watch for electric scooters sharing the bike lanes — they're quiet and fast.

## Small habits that save you
- **Keep your hotel's name and address in Chinese** in your notes — show it to drivers and staff.
- **Download offline maps** (Amap/Baidu) before you rely on them; indoor mall and station maps help underground.
- **Carry a little cash** for the rare vendor or machine that won't take a foreign card.
- **Metro before Didi in rush hour** — surface traffic in big cities crawls at peak.

## Accessibility & help
Major stations have elevators and staff; don't hesitate to ask uniformed staff ("工作人员") for the platform or exit. Most signage includes English in big-city metros, with Chinese always present.

Master the transit QR code and DiDi, and you'll go from "lost at the airport" to "navigating like a local" before your first noodle bowl.
