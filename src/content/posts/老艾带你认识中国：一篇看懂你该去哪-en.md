*China is vast—9.6 million square kilometers spanning five climate zones, from tropical islands to the “Roof of the World.” First-time visitors often stand before a map, unsure where to begin. This article won’t decide for you. It will simply help clarify one thing: *Where you go depends on what you want to see.*

> Hi, I’m Lao Ai. Over the years, I’ve traveled across China—from north to south, east to west—and visited most of its major cities. People often ask me: “China is so huge—where should I go first?” My answer is never just a list of place names. Instead, I always ask back: *Which China do you truly want to see?* This piece is a navigation map—for everyone still uncertain.

## Let’s Start with a Few Numbers  

Grasping these baseline figures gives you real coordinates for understanding *how large and rich China really is*:

* **Over 60 UNESCO World Heritage Sites** (reaching 60 after the Western Xia Tombs’ inscription in 2025; rising to 61 with Jingdezhen’s Traditional Porcelain Industry Heritage in 2026), including the world’s largest number of natural heritage sites;  
* **142 National Historical and Cultural Cities**, designated since the first batch of 24 in 1982—with additions every year;  
* **29 cities** with GDP exceeding 1 trillion RMB (2025 data), concentrated within the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, Yangtze River Delta, Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, and Chengdu-Chongqing city clusters;  
* **56 ethnic groups**, **1.4 billion people**, speaking over one hundred languages—from the eastern coast to the western frontiers.

Each number points to a distinct way of experiencing China. Below, we’ll organize destinations by *what you want to see*.

## I. The Chapter of Mountains and Rivers · To Witness Nature’s Masterpieces, Go Here  

![](/media/uploads/_来自小红书网页版.jpg)

The Chinese fascination with mountains and rivers is rare in the world—not about conquest, but pilgrimage. A single peak or river often carries millennia of aesthetic sensibility and philosophical depth. To experience *China’s natural beauty*, follow landform types—it’s the most efficient path.

**Mountains have their own lineages.** The Five Great Mountains are ritual coordinates of imperial Fengshan ceremonies: Mount Tai (East) embodies *magnificence*; Mount Hua (West), *perilous grandeur*; Mount Heng (South), *graceful elegance*; Mount Heng (North), *serene seclusion*; and Mount Song (Central), *dignified majesty*. Beyond them, Mount Huangshan stands alone with its “Five Wonders”—bizarre pines, grotesque rocks, sea of clouds, hot springs, and winter snow. As the saying goes: *“After returning from the Five Great Mountains, no other mountain seems worth seeing; after returning from Huangshan, even the Five Great Mountains pale.”* This isn’t hyperbole—it’s distilled aesthetic wisdom. Further southwest, Mount Emei’s refinement, Wuyi’s red-cliff landscapes, and Mount Lu’s misty veils each offer unique pathways into nature’s artistry.

**Rivers carry distinct personalities.** The Yangtze River stretches ~6,300 km—the Three Gorges section, with cliffs soaring straight up, delivers nature’s most eloquent argument. The Yellow River (~5,464 km) finds its iconic expression at Hukou Falls, where water surges and recoils—a visceral, primal image of *power*. For ink-wash poetry made real, head to the Li River: the stretch from Guilin to Yangshuo, with karst peaks mirrored in winding waters, has remained unchanged for centuries.

**Geological wonders make unforgettable photos.** For karst landscapes, head southwest: Guilin’s peaks, Guizhou’s Wanfenglin, and Yunnan’s Stone Forest form the world’s largest contiguous karst region. For Danxia landforms—vibrant, layered red sandstone—visit Zhangye’s Rainbow Danxia (a palette spilled across the earth), Guangdong’s Danxiashan, or Guizhou’s Chishui. For wind-eroded desolation, explore Wuerhe Devil City in Xinjiang.

**Terraced fields: where human hands collaborate with nature.** Yuanyang Hani Terraces (Yunnan), Longji Terraces (Guangxi), and Jiabang Terraces (Guizhou)—the Hani people sculpted hillsides into shimmering mirrors over 1,000 years. They are living proof of the philosophy *“harmony between heaven and humanity.”*

**Plateaus and snow-capped realms.** Qinghai Lake, Namtso, and Yamdrok Tso—high-altitude lakes so intensely blue they seem unreal. Ascend further: the Tibetan Plateau, Meili Snow Mountains, Gongga Mountain, and Mount Everest define the scale of the *“Roof of the World.”*

**Coasts and islands.** In the north, Qingdao and Dalian offer rocky shores and European-style historic districts; in the south, Xiamen’s piano-shaped island exudes slow-paced charm; farther out, Hainan and the Xisha Islands boast turquoise waters and powdery white sands—the tropical face of China.

> **Lao Ai’s personal note:** Don’t just tick off scenic spots. After Huangshan, you’ll understand why *“negative space”* is China’s highest aesthetic ideal. Stand awhile at Hukou Falls on the Yellow River—you’ll grasp why this river is called *“Mother River.”* In China, nature is never merely scenery.

## II. The Chapter of Pulse · To Experience Contemporary China, Go Here  

![](/media/uploads/上海｜7个视角承包魔都浪漫_1_不会拍照的小胡_来自小红书网页版.jpg)

To grasp *why today’s China looks the way it does*, the fastest route is to observe how its cities grew. By 2025, 29 Chinese cities had crossed the 1-trillion-RMB GDP threshold—not cold statistics, but a map to China’s development logic.

**Super-first-tier cities represent four distinct models.** Beijing—center of politics, culture, and technology—surpassed 5 trillion RMB in GDP in 2025, becoming the second city (after Shanghai) to clear that milestone. Shanghai leads with ~5.67 trillion RMB, serving as China’s financial and open-door window. Shenzhen *is* innovation itself—transformed from a fishing village to a global tech hub in just forty years. Guangzhou, a millennium-old commercial capital and maritime Silk Road gateway, remains a vital trade portal.

**City clusters are the true engines.** The Yangtze River Delta hosts 10 “trillion-RMB cities” (Shanghai, Hangzhou, Ningbo, Suzhou, Nanjing, Wuxi, Hefei, etc.), forming a global advanced manufacturing base—its integrated circuit industry accounts for over 60% of China’s total output. The Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area contributes 4 (Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Foshan, Dongguan), with clearly defined innovation roles. The Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei cluster centers on the capital’s coordinated development. Chengdu-Chongqing—the inland opening frontier—has emerged as a new highland: Chengdu posted 5.8% GDP growth in 2025, ranking among the nation’s top performers.

**Frontiers of openness deserve dedicated visits.** Hainan Free Trade Port, Shanghai Pudong, and Shenzhen Qianhai aren’t about sightseeing—they’re about witnessing *how policy transforms skylines.*

**Markets themselves have become attractions.** This is one of the newest phenomena: more and more foreigners come not just for tourism—but for *procurement*. Yiwu International Trade City welcomed 681,000 inbound business travelers in 2025—the world’s largest small-commodity market—making “sourcing tourism” a fresh trend. In Shenzhen’s Huaqiangbei, ~7,000 foreign buyers swarm daily—the continent’s biggest electronics distribution hub, where smartphones, drones, and AI hardware are freely traded. Guangzhou hosts over 500 specialized wholesale markets—covering apparel, cosmetics, and luggage. Lecong in Foshan is the world’s furniture distribution center. At Hengdian Film & TV City, inbound tourism surged 245% in the first three quarters of 2026—foreign visitors arriving to film short dramas inspired by *Empresses in the Palace*. Seeing China isn’t only about landmarks—step into a market, and you’ll encounter a livelier, steamier, more vivid China.

> **Lao Ai’s personal note:** Don’t limit urban exploration to shopping malls. Ride the subway during morning rush hour. Watch containers move at a port. Look up at city lights at night—that’s the physical embodiment of *“development.”* When I show China to foreign friends, this is what I love sharing most. And don’t skip wholesale markets: standing in a Yiwu shop, watching negotiators from 100 countries haggle—this visceral sense of *“the globe woven into one net”* is something no landmark can deliver.

## III. The Chapter of Cultural Continuity · To Encounter Millennia of Legacy, Go Here  

![](/media/uploads/西安不只有不夜城｜真正的长安在这里_3_一起发现美丽中国_来自小红书网页版.jpg)

China is the world’s only ancient civilization never interrupted. To feel *the weight of time*, focus on two axes: **ancient capitals** and **cultural hubs**.

**Ancient capitals are dynastic timelines.** Xi’an—capital of thirteen dynasties and eastern terminus of the Silk Road—holds the Terracotta Warriors just outside the city walls. Luoyang—also home to thirteen dynasties—boasts the Longmen Grottoes and peonies as its twin emblems. Nanjing—city of six dynasties’ mist and romance—still preserves the Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum and city walls. Kaifeng—capital of eight dynasties—features “cities stacked upon cities” underground, layer upon layer of imperial history. Hangzhou—Southern Song dynasty’s provisional capital—turned politics into poetry through West Lake. Beijing—imperial seat of Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties—culminates in the Forbidden City, the apex of its central axis.

**Cultural hubs are civilization’s workshops.** Qufu—Confucius’s hometown—hosts the “Three Kongs” (Temple of Confucius, Kong Family Mansion, and Cemetery of Confucius), the sacred heart of Confucianism. Jingdezhen—the porcelain capital for a millennium—turned clay into the East’s enduring vision of *“beautiful vessels.”* Suzhou—birthplace of classical gardens and Kunqu opera—proves *“Above heaven, below Suzhou and Hangzhou”* is no marketing slogan. Pingyao—a Ming-Qing dynasty county town—preserves China’s earliest “Wall Street” (draft banks) intact. Huizhou—its whitewashed walls and black-tiled roofs encode the grammar of traditional Chinese architecture. Datong—home to the Yungang Grottoes and Hanging Temple—reveals the bold ambition of the Northern Wei dynasty. Quanzhou—designated by UNESCO as the Maritime Silk Road’s starting point—holds half the world’s merchant ships within a single city.

**Two “invisible lines” also shape China’s story.** The Great Wall (east-west) and the Grand Canal (north-south) inscribe imperial defense and grain transport onto geography. Academies like Yuelu and Bailudong enshrine *“scholarship”* as sacred practice.

> **Lao Ai’s personal note:** The Chinese worldview is written into houses and cities. *“Center is supreme”* is visible in the Forbidden City’s central axis; *“harmony between heaven and humanity”* appears in Huizhou villages’ carefully designed water outlets. Don’t rush through ancient architecture—stand still for a moment. That’s more valuable than any photo.

## IV. The Chapter of Diversity · To Witness Ethnic Integration, Go Here  

![](/media/uploads/微信图片_20261004150743_76_1308.jpg)

China is not monochrome. Fifty-six ethnic groups—and over one hundred languages—thrive especially vividly in western and frontier regions. To experience *living diversity*, head toward these directions:

**Southwest China is the most vibrant scroll.** Xishuangbanna (Yunnan): Dai people’s Water-Splashing Festival (every April) turns entire streets into joyful water battles. Dali: Bai people live harmoniously with Cangshan Mountain and Erhai Lake. Diqing Shangri-La: Tibetans and Naxi share guardianship of Meili Snow Mountains. Qiandongnan (Guizhou): Xijiang Thousand-Household Miao Village’s stilted houses and silver ornaments are living specimens of Miao-Dong civilization. Honghe Hani Terraces: a love letter to mountains, penned by the Hani people.

**Northwest China embodies vastness and faith.** Kashgar (Xinjiang): Uyghur old town and bazaars remain vibrantly alive; Tianchi Lake, Kanas, Hemu Village, and Sayram Lake compose a sweeping autumn panorama across northern Xinjiang. Lhasa (Tibet): The Potala Palace and Jokhang Temple pulse as the heart of Tibetan culture; Linzhi’s snow-capped peaks and alpine lakes offer gentler grandeur. Hulunbuir (Inner Mongolia): Yurts, Naadam Festival (July–August), and horse-head fiddles speak the grammar of grasslands.

**Zoom in further:** Zhuang song festivals in Guangxi (on the third day of the third lunar month); Yi torch festivals in Liangshan (Sichuan); Korean-Chinese cultural life in Yanbian (Jilin); Tujia traditions in Xiangxi (Hunan)—*festivals are the best keys to unlock these places.*

> **Lao Ai’s personal note:** Many wonder: How do 56 ethnic groups and 1.4 billion people cohere as one? Attend a Water-Splashing Festival—or a Naadam. You’ll understand: unity doesn’t require sameness—it’s built on *inviting each other into our homes.* This is the deepest truth about China I wish every foreign friend could take away.
